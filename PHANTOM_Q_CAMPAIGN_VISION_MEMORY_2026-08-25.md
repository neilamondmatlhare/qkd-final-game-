## Product destination

Phantom Q Engineers is a persistent, professional cybersecurity and quantum-engineering learning game. A learner joins a believable organisation, moves through its real HQ, operates systems, witnesses consequences, handles evidence and earns practical authority. It is not a menu of mini-lessons and not a one-room QKD visualisation.

The experience combines:

- a raised-oblique, readable 3D HQ with cutaway behaviour and direct contextual movement;
- a campaign in which security concepts become needed because an operation requires them;
- Alice, Bob, Eve, HQ and Red Team as persistent operational roles with separate knowledge;
- a future physical-QKD and local-authoritative-simulation connection, where Unity presents verified state rather than inventing protocol truth;
- Guided, independent, classroom/host, practice, competition and later cooperative role-based modes without weakening role-safe information boundaries.

The UX reference is clear, professional and evidence-led: clean Phantom Q identity, contextual operational displays, readable data comparisons and serious security workspaces. The 3D world must carry the experience; flat slide-like panels are supporting interfaces, not a replacement for rooms, people and actions. Lighting should use controlled contrast and legible department identity, not blown-out white emissive floors.

## Campaign spine

| Segment | What the learner does | Lasting lesson / state |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Prologue - First Shift | Arrives at HQ, registers a fictional trainee identity, completes Eve's authorised credential check, receives initial clearance, logs into Central Operations Workstation 04, helps route normal transfers, then discovers a changed third file and opens PQ-001. | Normal behaviour is established before uncertainty. The learner reports evidence, not an attacker. |
| Incident 01 - Understand the Incident | Reconstructs the timeline, separates fact from assumption, checks source/transfer/receiver evidence, identifies CIA effects, reviews credential activity and builds/tests a verification process. | The learner can state what is known, what is affected and what remains unproven. Crypto access becomes meaningful. |
| Incident 02 - Protect the Information | Uses the Cryptography Lab to make a shared secret (A7), protect information symmetrically, send it, and discover that Bob cannot use the ciphertext without the secret. Explores controlled key-delivery choices. | Symmetric protection works, but shared-secret distribution is an operational problem. |
| Incident 03 - Complete the Symmetric Link | Verifies Bob's identity, authenticates and authorises key release, confirms matching A7, decrypts and compares original/recovered information, then completes a clean R100 training-payment practical. | A complete trusted symmetric workflow is demonstrated and saved as a baseline. It must succeed cleanly before the campaign breaks it. |
| Incident 04 - Trusted Workflow Breaks | Alice sends a legitimate request; Bob receives and processes a different valid-looking protected instruction. The learner compares evidence, reconstructs the channel timeline, tests passive observation versus replacement, finds unauthorised valid use of A7, revokes A7 and contains the impact. | Possession of a shared key is not proof of origin. Integrity/origin and financial consequence matter. A7 stays compromised; R1 000 is missing; the actor is still unknown. |
| Incident 05 - Rebuild Trust for Recovery | Treats `172.18.44.7` only as a network lead, brings Red Team to standby, creates/registers Red Team public/private capability, encrypts the recovery brief for Red Team, verifies HQ signatures, and establishes fresh symmetric session key S1 using asymmetric protection. | Network evidence is not attribution; public-key encryption and signatures solve different problems; secure traffic is not invisible traffic. Red Team is prepared but cannot self-authorise an attack. |
| Incident 06 - Recovery Operation | Verifies HQ's signed recovery order, follows the validated infrastructure lead, correlates evidence, requests target-specific approval, conducts fictional controlled access, preserves/signs evidence, traces and contains the financial route, requests recovery, restores the environment and verifies restoration. | Authority, scope, evidence preservation and recovery are all required. R1 000 can be recovered while the unknown Phantom remains unresolved. |

The knowledge curve is deliberate: ordinary operation -> uncertainty -> disciplined evidence -> symmetric protection -> trusted success -> compromise -> asymmetric trust and fresh session -> authorised recovery. Do not collapse these incidents into a lecture or solve later questions early.

## Prologue lock for Build 03

Build 03 is the complete Prologue vertical slice inside the existing HQ. Its required narrative anchors are:

1. exterior/arrival -> Reception;
2. registration and fictional trainee identity;
3. Eve's authorised adversarial credential check, with no suggestion that she is a villain;
4. initial clearance visibly applied at the access gate;
5. login at Central Operations WS04;
6. two routine transfer outcomes that create a normal baseline;
7. a third transfer that is not received as Alice expects, then an altered-file confirmation;
8. an evidence and credential activity check that preserves uncertainty;
9. opening PQ-001 and handing the learner to Foundations/Incident 01.

The Prologue must show changing HQ ambience and access through action. It must not reveal a hacker, malware, raw exploit details, a QKD solution, private role state or Eve's guilt. The correct result is a careful incident report with open questions.

## Campaign interaction rules

- Teach through objects and consequences: inspect -> predict -> configure -> run -> compare -> decide -> explain.
- A wrong order, unsupported claim or weak security choice produces an operational consequence, evidence gap or blocked authority - not a generic red “wrong” banner.
- Keep durable artefacts: case boards, timelines, verified workflows, key state, access decisions, evidence packages and incident state persist across the campaign.
- Separate source, transfer and receiver evidence. “Readability”, “identity”, “authentication”, “authorisation”, “integrity”, “origin” and “confidentiality” are not interchangeable.
- A key being possessed or a message decrypting does not prove its claimed author. A successful recovery does not prove the actor's identity.
- Model offensive activity only as fictional, simplified, target-specific and explicitly authorised. No real exploit strings, CVEs, shell commands, privilege escalation or unrestricted attack controls.

## HQ and progression relationship

Central Operations and WS04 are the player anchor. The campaign should physically move through the one HQ rather than load disconnected rooms:

`Exterior/Reception -> Central Ops -> Foundations -> Cryptography -> Communications -> SOC -> Red Team -> Engineering/Quantum/Advanced Compute/Secure Core as capability and future-story context require.`

The spaces have distinct recurring jobs:

- Reception/Identity: arrival, registration, initial access.
- Central Operations: persistent operational home, WS04, case status and campaign coordination.
- Foundations: evidence timeline, fact-versus-assumption and incident reasoning.
- Cryptography: symmetric protection, controlled key release, asymmetric/signature workbench.
- Communications: source/transfer/receiver path and remote Bob interface.
- SOC: alerting, traces, case correlation and increasing operational pressure.
- Red Team: professional authorised security-test and recovery capability, not a villain space.
- Engineering and Quantum: visible current/future capability, including real QKD apparatus/diagnostics when the campaign reaches it.
- Advanced Compute and Secure Core: advanced/future capability and restricted authority; not a substitute shortcut for the earlier campaign.

Clearance is multi-part: identity, role, demonstrated capability, zone permission, mission grant and action/target authority. Red Team access does not confer attack authority; HQ authorisation must be visible and verifiable.

## Characters and knowledge boundaries

- **Trainee/player:** begins with limited access and becomes an evidence-led operator. Competence, not menu selection alone, unlocks authority.
- **Alice:** sender/origin/protector and source of the legitimate original. Her source-of-truth relationship is critical in Incidents 03-04.
- **Bob:** receiver/verifier and destination witness. He is not incompetent; he accurately reports his available receiver-state evidence.
- **Eve:** authorised security support/adversarial tester. She can demonstrate observations and credential checks, but is never automatically the culprit.
- **HQ:** real operational authority, especially from Incident 05 onward. HQ signs/approves sensitive actions and must not be bypassed.
- **Red Team:** professional authorised operators. They prepare in Incident 05 and act only under a verified, target-specific mandate in Incident 06.
- **Unknown Phantom:** persistent unresolved adversarial presence represented through evidence and gaps, not an early visible person or automatic attribution.

For multiplayer, hidden information must remain filtered server-side until protocol or sealed review permits disclosure. Do not create a magical omniscient all-role screen.

## QKD position

QKD is a major long-term capability and must be technically honest. It is not an early shortcut that solves PQ-001 or replaces encryption/signatures/authorisation. The player first understands classical evidence, symmetric key distribution, authentication, origin and asymmetric trust. Later QKD establishes verified key material through authoritative simulation or physical Alice/Bob/Eve equipment; a later authenticated classical channel can use that material for an encrypted-file demonstration.

The Quantum Wing should therefore be visibly real from the beginning, but the Prologue-Incident 06 campaign need not pretend that every QKD feature is already operational.
